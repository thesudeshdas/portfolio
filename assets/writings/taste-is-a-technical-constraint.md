Good interfaces are not produced by adding polish after the difficult work is done. The visual decisions are part of the difficult work.

## The false handoff

Teams often describe design and engineering as two clean phases. One person decides what something should feel like. Another person translates that decision into code.

The translation is never neutral. A grid changes when real copy arrives. Motion changes when a device drops frames. Hierarchy changes when the most important action has the longest label.

> Taste is the ability to notice when a technically valid result is still the wrong result.

## Constraints create the character

A good constraint removes weak options. It can be as small as limiting a page to one type family, or as structural as refusing to hide product complexity behind a generic dashboard.

The point is not fewer choices. The point is better choices.

## What I now review

- Does the first screen communicate one clear idea?
- Does motion explain a state change?
- Does the layout survive real content?
- Can keyboard and touch users complete the same path?
- Is every visual effect worth its rendering cost?

Engineering does not begin after taste. Taste is one of its inputs.
