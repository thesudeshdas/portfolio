Some software becomes a product. Some software stays a private conversation between a problem and the person who keeps running into it.

![A quiet music cover used as temporary article artwork](/images/music/natural-light.jpg)

## No market required

Personal tools can be specific without apology. They can assume my habits, use my language, and solve the awkward edge cases that would never survive a product roadmap meeting.

That specificity is useful. It removes the pressure to turn every experiment into a startup pitch.

## The useful loop

I notice friction. I build the smallest response. I use it until the idea proves itself or becomes irrelevant.

```ts
const nextVersion = friction > curiosity ? 'delete' : 'keep building';
```

Most attempts should disappear. The few that stay become an honest record of how I work.

## A different measure

The success metric is not users, revenue, or a launch post. It is whether the tool changes my next Tuesday.
