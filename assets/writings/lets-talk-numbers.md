back in 2021 when I was living off my parents during my college days, I learnt something simple that has helped me earn 20 times more today (yes, this number is exaggerated, XD)

> side story, when I turned 18, my Baba took me to HDFC bank to open an account in my name. on the way, I don’t remember exactly, he told me this, “son, I don’t have wealth or land to pass, nor do I have financial wisdom that I can pass on. all I will say is save some money for rainy days and take the best decisions that you can take with the money that you earn”. this helped me accept my reality from my early days of being an adult to be mindful of my money and accept that I need to struggle to become rich

this led to me trying every trick in the book to learn and earn whatever money I could back in the day and freelancing was one of them. I started a small agency with two of my friends (huge shoutout to Sumitra and Suraj for kicking off that journey that led to where I am today) and made a few bucks; kidding, it was Indian Rupees, if only I earned in US Dollars 😭

the payout for us was in august 2021, mere ₹ 3,000 as an advance for the very first paid project (₹ 1,000 as my part). coming from a commerce background (yes, I am not an engineer by education), I instantly knew that I have to track this income. tried a bunch of apps before deciding on [**Money Manager**](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.realbyteapps.moneymanagerfree&pcampaignid=web_share) (here’s to half a decade of our relationship and to decades more 🥂).

it took some painful long hours, weeks probably, to get into the habit of tracking my income and expenses. can’t praise Money Manager enough for making this seamless. anyways, fast forward 5 years, i have gotten into the habit of

- tracking every single paise that I earn or spend
- weekly balancing the Money Manager app and my bank accounts (need some peace of mind that my tracking is on points)
- monthly budgeting for the next month based on the previous month cashflows

this helps me have a peace of mind, keep myself grounded on what I can or cannot do in the near future and sleep better at night

here are some crazy (and boring) stats

- I have spent around ₹ 1.8 lakhs on junk food
- motorcycling is even costlier than that, more than twice. but that’s what money is for, get that bike
- my salary has sextupled from february 2023 (that’s when I started my job)
- january and july are the most expensive months. january because most annual payments and july because birthday
- roughly one-sixth of everything I have earned has gone straight to rent (shoutout to Bangalore)

few learnings over these 5 years

first; a wise man once told there is money in job too, don’t think business is the only way. I too, as a young fool dreamt of never working and only hustling as a businessman. important context, I have the utmost respect for people who choose not to go the job route and do their own thing, more power to you, but it’s not for everyone. getting a job, working heads down and approaching your career as the athlete you worship (for me, it’s Cristiano Ronaldo) is a sure shot way of earning loads (okay, maybe not loads) of money.

mind you, I am not saying I am rich, I am saying I am less poorer today than I was yesterday. get that job, stick it out, give it your all. whatever money I earn and spend today (read spend as in go on trips, buy things, take care of my family, and much more) is possible only because of my job

second; the only people who say money doesn’t bring happiness fall into the following three categories

1. too rich to understand it’s value
2. too foolish to comprehend what money actually does
3. too lazy to earn

distance yourself from all three. you become what you surround yourself with. company matters a lot more than your own capability. a simpler example will be imagine you are a top top athlete, but your surround yourself with your drunkard friends, all unfit and unwilling to ever pick up sports. do you think you can ever become a professional athlete?

third; saving is overrated. there are two types of people that I have observed in my short lived life. ones who live for tomorrow and ones who live for today. I will save/invest all my money versus YOLO, let me spend it all today. both are equally wrong and bad.

> you cannot build wealth with ₹ 5,000 SIP
>
> — some wise man (not me)

fourth; having money and peace of mind unlocks something in you that I cannot explain. I will try though. you perform better at job, you get into better places, you hang out with better people and you get better opportunities

and lastly; do not listen to anyone about your money. it’s your money. let me emphasise

it is YOUR money

YOU earned it, after long painful hours. YOU decide what you want to do. after all, we have to bear the consequences of our own actions anyways, why let someone else dictate my your actions then?

ps - your circumstances are different than mine, my advises are based on my circumstances. take what’s of worth, leave what’s not

until next time, fin
